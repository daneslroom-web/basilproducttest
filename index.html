Safety & escalation

The procedure is fixed. Your data and your kitchen's process are not.

The safety flow

Locked — industry standard
  1. Acknowledge the allergy out loud
  2. Never guess
  3. Check against confirmed data
  4. Escalate if anything is unconfirmed
  5. Confirm back to the guest before the order is sent
This cannot be edited, shortened or turned off. It is the same in every venue on Basil, and it is what your team is scored against.

Your escalation procedure

Staff hear this back from Marco during practice, so write it the way you would say it on the floor.

The matrix

A server may offer this
Needs your authorisation
Passing on your decision is always allowed. Once a server has come to you and you have authorised something, they may tell the guest — "the manager has said he'll take that off the bill." That is correct service and is scored as correct. It is only a failure when a server commits to it themselves.

Gesture cap

Decision 2 Set the cap as money or as a percentage?
€
%
A fixed amount, because a server has to apply it in their head with a guest waiting. "Up to fifteen euros" is a rule someone can follow at a table. "Up to 10%" is arithmetic on a bill they have not seen yet, and it quietly gives a big table more authority than a small one — which is backwards.

Payment policy

Everything about the bill. This is the part of your service no AI can guess correctly.

For Tom: new entity, and the injectable block for Module 4. Highest fabrication risk in the product — service charge, cover, corkage and tipping norms vary by venue and by country, and a model will state a plausible answer with total confidence. The anti-fabrication guardrail goes into Module 4's Marco from the first line, not retrofitted.
